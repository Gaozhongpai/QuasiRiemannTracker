\begin{abstract}
We sharpen the all-moduli Dirichlet nonvanishing boundary from $0.874840$
to $0.874836$. The new ingredient is a joint estimate that preserves the
total logarithmic width of the selected primes through both allocation
stages and couples the resulting saving to a valuation constraint on the
actual numerator. Keeping this moving width, rather than its smallest
admissible value, gives a stronger affine moment region. Combining the
estimate with the existing bounds for the remaining source and exceptional
terms yields the moment inequality needed for the zero-free region.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and proof strategy}
\begin{theorem}
For every positive integer $n$, every Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $n$,
and every $z\in\mathbb C$,
\[
 \Re z>\tht:=\frac{218709}{250000}=0.874836,
 \qquad \neg(\chi=1\ \text{and}\ z=1)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(\chi,z)\ne0.                 \tag{1}
\]
\end{theorem}
The analytic framework is inherited from OpenAI and Collet
\cite{openai,collet}. It studies a global zero supremum $\beta$ in an
Eisenstein Hecke family and transfers its bound to all Dirichlet characters.
We use the established $\beta\le0.874840$ \cite{incumbent} as an input.
The new estimate improves the moment calculation inside that argument;
it does not replace the underlying zero-density or continuation theorems.

Here is the moment region that the argument needs. Normalize all logarithmic
lengths by $\log U$, where $U>1$ is the source scale. The two ordinary
Dirichlet-polynomial lengths are $m_1,m_2$, and
$s=\sum_j\log \mathrm N(P_j)/\log U$ is the total width of the selected prime ideals $P_j$.
Write $\mathcal E_\eta(\Phi,\mathbf p)$ for the normalized energy in Collet's initial affine
moment, with fixed character $\eta$, shifts of size at most $T$, and the
original masks and selected-prime weights.

\begin{proposition}[Affine moment region]
For every $\varepsilon>0$, the initial energy satisfies
\[
 \mathcal E_\eta(\Phi,\mathbf p)\ll_{\eta,\varepsilon}
       \mathcal D(\Phi)\mathcal S(\mathbf p)^2(1+T)^N U^{1+\varepsilon}
 \quad\text{for}\quad m_1+m_2+\lambda s\le B,              \tag{2}
\]
where
\[
 \lambda=\frac{19793}{5000},\qquad B=\frac{987047}{1000000},\qquad
 \frac{41}{100}\le m_i\le\frac{11}{25},\quad
 \frac{17}{500}\le s\le\frac1{25}.
\]
Here $\Phi$ is a nonnegative radial weight, $\mathbf p$ a pair of test
functions, $\mathcal D$ the standard diagonal control, $\mathcal S$ a finite
Schwartz control, and $N$ a finite degree. The prime-factor partition is
sufficiently fine, as in the initial-moment theorem.
\end{proposition}
The significance of (2) is its available prime width: for equal plain
lengths $m$, it permits $s\le(B-2m)/\lambda$. This width later enters the
count of detector rows. A saving lost at the moment stage therefore cannot
be recovered merely by changing the detector parameters.

The source decomposition has a LOW part handled by the incumbent moment,
and a HIGH part requiring marked counting. The new part is a width-sensitive
estimate for the latter. After the two source
allocations, some selected width has entered common factors, some is frozen,
and the rest survives. We keep their sum tied to the original width and
use the numerator valuation bound before separating these contributions.
Section~2 proves the resulting joint inequality. Section~3 combines it with
the known LOW estimate and the error terms to prove Proposition~1.
Section~4 applies this moment to the detector and weighted numerator,
then invokes the inherited continuation argument to prove Theorem~1.

\section{The joint HIGH estimate}
\subsection*{The numerator and the common factors}
Let $\nu$ denote the auxiliary density level of a HIGH source bin; its
zero-real-part threshold is $(1+\nu)/2$. Put
\[
 \rho(\nu)=1-\frac{10\nu}{3(5-2\nu)},\qquad
 \gamma(\nu)=\nu-
       \max\left(4\rho(\nu)-1,\frac{2+22\rho(\nu)}{21}\right). \tag{3}
\]
Here $\rho$ is the exponent supplied by the unmarked source estimate.
In the range used below, $1/4\le\rho\le2/5$.

The first allocation has logarithmic ideal lengths $c,d$, active radical
width $R$, cancelled radical width $R_0$, and numerator width $L_c$.
The nonnegative quantity $E$ is its inactive-projector loss. These quantities
belong to the same allocation. Besides $R\le\min(c,d)$ and $R_0\ge0$,
the actual numerator satisfies
\[
 L_c\le5(R-R_0),\qquad 4L_c\le5(c+d).                    \tag{4}
\]
The second inequality follows prime by prime from the numerator valuations
and holds in either reflection orientation. Its use together with the
first inequality, rather than as an independent bound on a free numerator,
is the relevant coupling.

For $x_+=\max(x,0)$, the common-factor part of the HIGH exponent is
\[
 \mathcal F=(1-\rho)(c-d)_+-\nu c-(1+\rho)R-E
             -\frac{(3c-5d-R)_+}{6}+\rho L_c+R_0.
\]
We obtain
\[
                   \mathcal F\le-\gamma(\nu)c-E.         \tag{5}
\]
For completeness, when $c\le d$, insert $L_c\le5(R-R_0)$ and $R\le c$;
this gives rate $\nu-(4\rho-1)$. When $d\le c$, a positive linear
combination of (4) gives
\[
 21\rho L_c+21R_0\le(19\rho-4)(c+d)+(16+29\rho)R.
\]
Insert this and retain the positive-part penalty in $\mathcal F$; the
resulting rate is $\nu-(2+22\rho)/21$. Taking the smaller of these two
rates proves (5). The two-piece rate is essential to cover both orderings.

\subsection*{Width survives both allocations}
Let $u,f,v\ge0$ be the common width removed by the first allocation,
the frozen width in the second, and the surviving selected width.
Let $Q$ be the remaining logarithmic frequency scale. The source identities
give bounds of the form
\[
                 u+f+v\ge S,\qquad Q\ge F-2u.            \tag{6}
\]
The common width pays rate $\gamma=\gamma(\nu)$ by (5), the second allocation
pays rate $\nu$, and the marked inverse/plain estimate of timbalud
\cite{asymmetric} pays
\[
 r(\nu)\min(v,\tau_sQ),\qquad
 r(\nu)=\frac{32}{39}\frac{\nu(5-6\nu)}{5-2\nu},\quad
 \tau_s=\frac{13}{400}.
\]
Thus the joint payment is $\gamma u+\nu f+r\min(v,\tau_sQ)$.

\begin{lemma}[Conserved-width payment]
If $S,F\ge0$, $0\le\gamma\le\nu$, $r\ge0$, $\tau_s\ge0$ and
$2r\tau_s\le\gamma$, then (6) implies
\[
 \gamma u+\nu f+r\min(v,\tau_sQ)
       \ge\min\{\gamma S,\ rS,\ r\tau_sF\}.             \tag{7}
\]
\end{lemma}
If $v\le\tau_sQ$, compare the three width rates and use $u+f+v\ge S$.
Otherwise use $Q\ge F-2u$: the coefficient of $u$ is
$\gamma-2r\tau_s\ge0$, leaving at least $r\tau_sF$.
The large sixth-power tail has payment $\gamma u+\nu f+tQ$,
where $t=(6\rho-1)/250$; the same proof gives $tF$ when $2t\le\gamma$.
These inequalities keep the common and surviving widths in one estimate.
Replacing $S$ by its minimum prematurely discards saving precisely where
the amplifier and total source volume are also increasing.

\section{Completing the affine moment}
We now choose the amplifier while keeping the width variable.
Let $b$ be the upper endpoint of the cell containing $s$, with
$b-10^{-5}\le s\le b$; thus $3401/100000\le b\le1/25$.
Write $a(b)$ for the amplifier exponent and $A(b)$ for the total source-volume
exponent. Take
\begin{gather*}
 q=\frac{839}{1250},\qquad \kap_0=2(0.874840)-1=\frac{9371}{12500},
 \qquad r_L=\frac1{4000000},\\
 D(b)=B-1+(6\kap_0-\lambda)b,\qquad
 a(b)=\frac{qD(b)}{6(\kap_0-q)}+\frac{r_L}{1-q/\kap_0},\\
 A(b)=B-(\lambda-1)(b-10^{-5}),\qquad
 S(b)=b-10^{-5}-10^{-3},\qquad
 F(b)=\frac{122}{125}\bigl(1-6a(b)-10^{-3}\bigr).         \tag{8}
\end{gather*}
The formula for $A$ follows from
$m_1+m_2+s\le B-(\lambda-1)s$. The two allocations and the discarded tiny
slots give (6) with $S=S(b)$ and $F=F(b)$.
In particular the supply varies with the original width $s$.

\textbf{LOW.} The established incumbent is $\kap_0$, not the new value
$2\tht-1$ that we seek to prove. The exact identity
\[
 a(b)-\frac{q}{6\kap_0}\bigl(D(b)+6a(b)\bigr)=r_L        \tag{9}
\]
pays the complete LOW removal cost. Both its capacity and removal price
use the same incumbent. The original positive/zero moment estimates and
height bounds therefore apply without circular feedback. The LOW/HIGH
zero-bin junction is $(1+q)/2=2089/2500$.

\textbf{HIGH.} Before the width payment, the remaining saving is
\[
 H(b,\nu)=1-\rho(\nu)-\nu A(b)-a(b)(5-6\rho(\nu)).
\]
Combining this with (7) and the tail estimate reduces HIGH to four comparisons:
\begin{align*}
 H+\gamma S-\tfrac1{20000}-\tfrac1{200000}&\ge10^{-6},&
 H+rS-\tfrac1{20000}-\tfrac1{200000}&\ge10^{-6},\\
 H+r\tau_sF-\tfrac1{20000}-\tfrac1{200000}&\ge10^{-6},&
 H+tF-\tfrac1{20000}-\tfrac1{200000}&\ge10^{-6}.          \tag{10}
\end{align*}
The $1/200000$ term reserves the raw-root charge in the marked estimate;
the unmarked common-width estimate can omit it.
These inequalities hold for every $b$ in its cell range and
$q\le\nu\le7/10$. At $\nu=q$ each is affine in $b$, so rational arithmetic
at the two endpoints suffices. To extend in $\nu$, $H$ increases by at least
$(\nu-q)/5$, $\gamma$ is nondecreasing, and $r,t$ decrease by at most
$\nu-q$ and $(\nu-q)/20$, respectively. Use
$S,\tau_sF\le1/25$ and $F\le1$. For $7/10\le\nu\le3/4$ the unmarked
estimate already gives $H\ge10^{-6}$.

\textbf{ERROR and the remaining terms.} Error deletion retains the original
$3/4$ incumbent and hence the coefficient $9/2$, independently of (9).
Its defect and first-branch saving are
\[
 D_E(b)=B-1+(9/2-\lambda)b,\qquad
             a(b)/4-D_E(b)/6\ge1/1000.                 \tag{11}
\]
The sixth and seventh deletion branches have separate positive reserves.
All three exceed the pre-loss reserve $7/30000000$; after branch-loss
absorption the normalized physical sum uses saving $7/60000000$, with
allocation and shell losses still separate. The moving-prime average is
paid once and the retained radical witness is preserved.
Finally $1-A(b)-5a(b)\ge227876729/3446640000$ pays the zero term; the
amplifier coefficient is at most $2048$, and the inherited tail estimates
complete the sum. The original source decomposition, with both reflections
and all shifted heights, now proves Proposition~1.

\section{From the moment to the nonvanishing boundary}
Let $\delta$ now denote the \emph{outer detector} density; it is independent
of the inner source parameter $\nu$ in Sections~2--3.
For inverse length $r$, plain length $m$, cutoff $t=r+m$ and normalized
amplitude $p\in[0,1/2]$, the three count exponents are
\[
 I=1-\delta\bigl(p+(1-p)r\bigr),\quad
 P=1-2\delta m-2\delta p\frac{B-2m}{\lambda},\quad
 L=1-\delta+(5/6-\delta)(t-1).
\]
The new width in $P$ is exactly Proposition~1. Balancing $I,P,L$ gives
\begin{gather*}
 D_p=3-p-4p/\lambda,\quad a_p=2-4p/\lambda,\quad c_p=1-2pB/\lambda,\\
 J=(5/6-\delta)D_p+\delta(1-p)a_p,\qquad
 \Raff=1-\delta+\frac{(5/6-\delta)\delta(1-p)c_p}{J}.      \tag{12}
\end{gather*}
Following DaysSky \cite{detector}, apply the moment separately to each
character in the finite detector dictionary; maxima of the degrees and
unions of the seminorms give a common height range for that dictionary.
Use (12) on $[.451,.49]\times[.49,.5]$, the accepted affine count on
$[.445,.451]\times[.4975,.5]$, and the original count elsewhere.
The selected widths and plain lengths remain within the corresponding
moment windows; in particular $m\le847/2000$ on the new affine branch.

Choose the outer geometry
\[
 h=3181/4000,\quad d_-=h-3/5000,\quad d_+=h+10^{-10},\quad
 \ell=11/3-4\tht,\quad x=1+\ell-h,\quad y=h-2\ell,\quad
 \kap_*=2\tht-1.
\]
The lower and upper height ranges use capacities
\[
 C_O(d)=\frac{\min(d,2y-d)}{6\kap_*},\qquad
 C_N(d)=\frac{2y-(2-B)d}{\lambda}.
\]
Writing $R$ for the chosen count exponent, the weighted-numerator comparison is
\begin{align*}
 E(d,C,\delta,p)={}&\frac{1+\delta}{2}+\frac{13h}{75}-\frac y2-\frac\ell2
 +\ell\delta p+d\left(\frac14-\frac{17}{50}\right)
 -\frac{\delta pC}{2}+\frac{3dR}{4}
 \ \le\ \tht-10^{-7}.                                  \tag{13}
\end{align*}
This holds on the full rectangle $0\le\delta\le3/4$, $0\le p\le1/2$.
After multiplying by the positive count denominators, rational Bernstein
polynomials prove (13). Affinity in $d$ leaves five ends:
$1/200,y,d_-$ with $C_O$, and $d_-,d_+$ with $C_N$.
The shifted source uses $t_0=103/100000$; its large-shift and tail estimates
are included. Feedback inequalities extend the comparison from $\kap_*$
to the actual $\kap=2\beta-1$.

Levy's weighted numerator and Collet's continuation argument
\cite{akash,collet} now compare the lower and upper bounds for the same
normalized probe. If $\beta>\tht$, choose a common positive continuation
reserve and $\omega=(\beta-\tht)/2$ before the target characters.
The finite-dictionary moments above allow target-dependent height ranges,
while (13) supplies a common positive gap. Continuation then strictly
improves the global supremum $\beta$, a contradiction. The inherited
Hecke-to-Dirichlet transfer proves (1).
